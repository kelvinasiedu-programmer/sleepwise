Sources

Last reviewed: June 2026

SleepWise draws on public, government-maintained medical databases. Evidence shown on each result links back to one of these.

Primary sources

  • NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) - fact sheets on supplements such as melatonin, magnesium, and valerian. ods.od.nih.gov
  • MedlinePlus (U.S. National Library of Medicine) - consumer information on herbs and supplements. medlineplus.gov
  • NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) - evidence summaries for complementary approaches such as melatonin. nccih.nih.gov
  • openFDA - drug label and adverse-event data from the FDA. open.fda.gov
  • RxNorm (U.S. National Library of Medicine) - a planned source for future live medication-name resolution. The current version uses a limited offline medication map, not a live RxNorm lookup. nlm.nih.gov

How sources are used

The links on each result point to the general database reference for that supplement or drug class. They are not a claim-by-claim citation: a link means "this is the public database this topic is drawn from," not "this exact sentence and number appear on that page." Dose ranges and summaries are paraphrased to be consistent with these public references, but a precise, section-level citation and an independent clinical review of every claim are still pending. Until that review is done, treat everything here as general education, verify any specific figure against the linked source, and confirm with a pharmacist. Entries are tagged in the underlying data with whether they have been verified against their source.

What sources do not replace

These are reference materials, not personal medical advice. Your clinician or pharmacist has context that no public database can. See the Medical disclaimer.
