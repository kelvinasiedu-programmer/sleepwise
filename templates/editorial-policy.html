Editorial policy

Last reviewed: June 2026

SleepWise publishes health information, so this page spells out exactly how that content is produced, what standards it follows, and what its limits are.

How pages are produced

Every supplement and interaction page is generated from the same curated dataset that powers the checker. Each claim in that dataset carries a citation to a public, government-maintained medical source, and the page shows the citation next to the claim. Nothing on a content page is written freehand; if a statement is not in the sourced dataset, it does not appear on the site.

Sourcing standards

  • Primary sources are the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, MedlinePlus, and openFDA (see Sources).
  • Every interaction rule and evidence entry records its source URL.
  • Each entry carries a verification flag. Entries not yet re-checked against their source are marked unverified in the data and treated more cautiously.
  • Cautious language is enforced: pages never declare a combination "safe" and always route decisions to a pharmacist or clinician.

What we withhold

Each claim in the dataset records whether it has been confirmed against the source it cites. Claims we could not confirm are not published. Where a supplement has no confirmed evidence left, the page says so rather than filling the space with statements we cannot stand behind, and unconfirmed claims are excluded from the structured data search engines read.

Safety warnings are handled differently and deliberately. An interaction caution we have not yet confirmed is still shown, clearly labelled as precautionary, because quietly removing a plausible warning to tidy up a citation would make the tool less safe for the person who needs it. It is labelled, not hidden, and not presented as substantiated.

Review status

SleepWise has not yet been independently reviewed by a licensed clinician or pharmacist. That is disclosed here and on every content page rather than implied otherwise. When a professional review happens, reviewed pages will name the reviewer and their credentials, and nothing will carry a review stamp it did not earn. If you are a pharmacist or clinician willing to review the interaction table, see Contact.

Updates and corrections

  • Content pages show a last source-reviewed date.
  • Corrections can be reported through the public issue tracker; the data and its history are open for inspection.
  • Source material changes (for example, an updated NIH fact sheet) trigger a re-review of the entries that cite it.

Commercial disclosure

Supplement links are general search links, marked sponsored/no-follow, and are not currently monetized. No content is ranked or altered for payment, and if affiliate links are ever added, that will be disclosed on the page and in Privacy.
